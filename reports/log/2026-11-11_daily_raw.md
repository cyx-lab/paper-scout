**Title:** Evidence of anomalous Hall crystals in moiré rhombohedral graphene

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:berry curvature

**Authors:** Hongyuan Li, Zuhan Geng, Annika Weber, Chenxi Xu, Yifan Jiang, Jiabin Xie, Lujin Min, Kenji Watanabe, Takashi Taniguchi, Kenji Yasuda, Xiaomeng Liu, Jie Shan, Kin Fai Mak

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> An anomalous Hall crystal (AHC) is a quantum state of matter, in which spontaneous electron crystallization leads to a quantized Hall effect without a magnetic field. The state can emerge in materials with strong Coulomb interaction and large Berry curvatures, such as few-layer rhombohedral graphene. Although the idea of AHCs has been invoked to explain some of the observed Chern insulators in moiré rhombohedral graphene (mRG), direct experimental evidence of AHCs has been missing so far. Here, we report experimental evidence of AHCs in hole-doped mRG through combined electrical transport and noise measurements. We identify a series of distinct Chern insulators spanning a broad and continuous range of doping densities incommensurate with the underlying moiré lattice; the states exhibit current-induced broadband noise and memory effects as well as pronounced gate hysteresis. Systematic magnetic field, temperature and gate dependence studies further support the emergence of a spontaneously charge-ordered state that acts as a parental state for the observed Chern insulators. The absence of Chern insulators at nearby integer fillings also distinguishes the observed state from the reported reentrant or extended quantum (anomalous) Hall states. Altogether, our results establish experimental evidence of AHCs in mRG and open the door for further exploration of the collective dynamics in this new state of matter.

## Rank #2

**Title:** Structural and electronic properties of (Hf,Zr)O$_2$ phases from first principles

**Keyword hits:** exciton:exciton, exciton:excitonic

**Authors:** Sibghatullah Khan, Hannu-Pekka Komsa

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> (Hf,Zr)O$_2$ material system is promising for ferroelectric devices, owing to their compatibility with the existing electronics manufacturing processes. Technology computer-aided design (TCAD) simulations can be used for the device design and optimization, but the predictive power of TCAD device simulations depends on the quality of the material parameters. These are still surprisingly poorly understood, also for the metastable ferroelectric phase, largely owing to the difficulties in synthesizing phase-pure samples. Here, we carry out high-quality first-principles calculations to extract reliable lattice parameters, dielectric constants, and band gaps for six of the most common phases of HfO$_2$, Hf$_{0.5}$Zr$_{0.5}$O$_2$, and ZrO$_2$. We compare results from GGA, meta-GGA, and hybrid functionals to shed light on the sensitivity of the results on the level of theory and to connect the results to earlier literature. In the case of band structures, we also consider $GW$ corrections as well as the effects of temperature-induced band gap renormalization and excitonic effects. Finally, we use these results in TCAD simulations for (Hf,Zr)O$_2$ FeCAP to demonstrate the effect that non-ferroelectric interlayers could have on the hysteresis curves and band diagrams.

## Rank #3

**Title:** Hidden interfacial magnetism in RuO$_2$ heterostructures revealed by a superconducting spin switch

**Keyword hits:** altermagnetism:altermagnetic

**Authors:** Tetsuma Mandokoro, Hisakazu Matsuki, Shutaro Karube, Rodrigo de las Heras, Andrei Mazanik, Akihiro Yamada, Fugo Tokoro, Susumu Hirata, Ryusuke Hisatomi, Yoichi Shiota, Vitaly N. Golovach, F. Sebastian Bergeret, Teruo Ono

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> RuO$_2$, once the flagship altermagnetic candidate, is now believed to be nonmagnetic in the bulk, yet weak, static and spatially inhomogeneous magnetism emerges at its surfaces and interfaces. Where this magnetism resides, what stabilises it, and whether it is connected to these states remain open questions. Here we address these questions at the Nb/RuO$_2$ interface using a superconducting spin switch. A GdN/Nb/RuO$_2$ trilayer allows interfacial exchange-field alignment to tune the superconducting transition temperature of Nb, probing interfacial magnetism inaccessible to conventional magnetometry. The switch reveals a hidden magnetic subsystem, which we attribute to a thin RuO$_x$ redox layer formed as Nb scavenges oxygen from RuO$_2$. This layer behaves as a weak and magnetically hard ferromagnet with vacancy-induced anisotropy. Furthermore, supercurrent amplifies the switching efficiency, making the probe sensitivity tunable in situ. Superconducting spin switches thus emerge as a quantitative low-temperature probe of hidden magnetism at buried interfaces.
