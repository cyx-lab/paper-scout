### Related topics

### One-sentence summary

Real-space calculations reveal that the anomalous Hall effect in rhombohedral graphene arises from interference between closed current paths under valley polarization rather than equilibrium loop currents.

### Problem

Whether the observed anomalous Hall effect and magnetization in rhombohedral graphene are accompanied by equilibrium loop currents or arise from interference between alternative propagation paths in closed current correlations.

### Approach

Projecting the Hamiltonian onto surface orbitals, performing a real-space expansion of the interior propagator, and computing current correlations and Hall conductivity across various reduced models.

### Main takeaway

Rhombohedral graphene in the uniform valley-polarized state exhibits no equilibrium bond currents; its anomalous Hall effect stems from interference between interlayer propagation paths forming Wilson-loop structures of current correlations.

### Why it matters

It clarifies the microscopic mechanism of the anomalous Hall response in multi-layer rhombohedral graphene and provides a theoretical foundation for distinguishing orbital responses in normal and superconducting states.

### Value assessment

- **Mechanism clarification**
- This work clarifies via real-space path analysis that the anomalous Hall effect in rhombohedral graphene originates from path interference rather than equilibrium loop currents.

### Related topics

quantum_geometry:berry curvature · quantum_geometry:nonlinear Hall · quantum_geometry:nonlinear Hall effect

### One-sentence summary

Breaking spatial inversion symmetry via arsenic substitution in borophosphene derivatives yields a robust nonlinear Hall response driven by orbital physics and a sizable Berry curvature dipole without requiring spin-orbit coupling.

### Problem

Realizing the nonlinear Hall effect in two-dimensional materials requires breaking spatial inversion symmetry, yet finding light-element, robust, and spin-orbit-coupling-free platforms with tunable Berry curvature dipoles remains a challenge.

### Approach

Using density functional theory calculations and tight-binding models constructed via maximally localized Wannier functions, the electronic structures, orbital couplings, and strain-tunable nonlinear Hall responses of As-substituted borophosphene monolayers were investigated.

### Main takeaway

As-substitution opens sizable band gaps (90–120 meV) and induces finite Berry curvature dipoles without spin-orbit coupling, while uniaxial compressive strain can amplify the dipole by over ten times and modulate its sign.

### Why it matters

This work establishes light-element borophosphene derivatives as promising, spin-orbit-coupling-free platforms for mechanically switchable nonlinear transport and symmetry-controlled quantum device applications.

### Value assessment

### Problem
