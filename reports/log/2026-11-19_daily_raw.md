## Rank #1

**Title:** Light-Induced Odd-Parity h-Wave Magnetism in Penrose Quasicrystals

**Keyword hits:** quasicrystal:quasicrystal, quasicrystal:quasicrystalline

**Authors:** Yiming Li, Huaqing Huang

**Author_match:** True

**Abstract:**
> Noncrystallographic rotational symmetry offers a route to unconventional spin splitting beyond the constraints of periodic crystals. We show that circularly polarized light induces odd-parity $h$-wave magnetism in a collinear antiferromagnetic Penrose quasicrystal without spin-orbit coupling. Light-induced chiral hopping, generated by virtual two-step processes, combines with staggered exchange to lift the spin degeneracy of the undriven system. Quasicrystalline spin-group symmetry enforces odd-parity spin splitting and selects $h$-wave as its leading angular harmonic. An analytical effective quasi-band theory relates this splitting to the geometry of local hopping paths and predicts its reversal upon switching the light helicity. Momentum-resolved spectral calculations reveal the characteristic alternating spin contrast, for which we propose spin-resolved time- and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy as an experimental probe. Our results establish a mechanism for optically controlling higher-harmonic odd-parity magnetism and demonstrate how quasicrystalline order extends Floquet engineering beyond crystallographic symmetry constraints.

## Rank #2

**Title:** From geometric curvature to effective backgrounds: a dictionary for Dirac fermions in one, two and three spatial dimensions

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:geometric contribution

**Authors:** David Valenzuela, Alfredo Raya

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Dirac equation on curved backgrounds can be rewritten as a flat-space equation in which geometry appears as a tensorial Fermi velocity together with a set of background fields. We derive this correspondence from a single Clifford-algebra identity and follow it through one, two, and three spatial dimensions. Once the spin connection is written in terms of Ricci rotation coefficients, only two contractions can occur. The trace produces a term with the index structure of a vector potential in every dimensionality; it enters with an imaginary factor i and is precisely the term that keeps the operator Hermitian when the Fermi velocity depends on position. The totally antisymmetric contraction produces an object that depends on the number of indices available to the Levi-Civita symbol: a trace on a line, a pseudoscalar mass on a surface, and an axial vector potential in a volume. Neither contraction is invariant under local rotations of the frame, and we give the transformation laws. On a surface, the curl of the trace term can always be removed by a frame rotation, while its divergence is proportional to the Gaussian curvature. We apply the correspondence to a Gaussian bump, where the local density of states at the center falls below its flat value and obeys a closed form at low energy; to a wire, where a nonuniform lapse binds states inside the gap and a nonuniform velocity cannot; to stationary frames on a surface, where a circulating drag shifts the Dirac mass locally with either sign; and to twisted and rotating frames in a volume, where the axial term takes the form of a chiral chemical potential produced by the frame alone, or separates Weyl nodes in a rotating sample. We separate the geometric contribution from the lattice pseudogauge field that dominates the response of strained samples

## Rank #3

**Title:** Time-Varying Medium from an Ensemble of Laser-Dressed Quantum Emitters

**Keyword hits:** exciton:polariton

**Authors:** Adrián Juan-Delgado, Diego Martín-Cano, Paloma A. Huidobro

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> We propose a minimal quantum-mechanical model of a frequency dispersive, time-modulated medium that explicitly incorporates the pumping mechanism. It consists of a homogeneous ensemble of quantum emitters dressed by a pump laser. We analyze their dispersive, time-varying response to a perturbative probe field by deriving the macroscopic polarizability of the ensemble, and then solving Maxwell's equations via the Floquet formalism. This yields a rich band structure that departs from conventional analyses in which the modulation source is neglected, arising from the interplay between the dispersive polaritonic and time-varying responses. These results shed light on the role of the modulation source in time-varying media and pave the way toward realizing dispersive time-varying media from driven quantum-mechanical systems.

## Rank #4

**Title:** Gas-induced reversible spectral tuning of bound states in the continuum in perovskite metasurfaces

**Keyword hits:** exciton:exciton, exciton:excitonic

**Authors:** Nikita Glebov, Yuliya Kenzhebayeva, Pavel Pankin, Mikhail Masharin, Martin Rojas Bustamante, Xiuzhen Liu, Shixiong Li, Ruslan Azizov, Dmitrii Maksimov, Hatice Altug, Andrey Bogdanov, Sergey Makarov

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Bound states in the continuum (BICs) provide a powerful route to engineering optical resonances in compact photonic structures, enabling enhanced light--matter interaction for sensing, nonlinear optics, optical modulation, and lasing. The next step towards practical BIC-based meta-optics is dynamic and reversible spectral control after fabrication. However, optical and electro-optical tuning typically produces limited refractive-index changes, thermo-optical approaches require continuous external actuation, and phase-change materials often provide discrete switching rather than continuous spectral reconfiguration. Here we demonstrate giant, fully reversible, and continuous spectral tuning of BIC resonances in halide-perovskite metasurfaces. Using nanoimprint lithography, we fabricate MAPbBr$_3$ metasurfaces, that support BIC-derived resonances in the visible spectral range. Exposure to HCl vapor initiates an anion-exchange reaction in which Cl$^-$ ions diffuse into the perovskite crystalline lattice and partially replace Br$^-$ ions, producing a monotonic increase of the electronic bandgap and blueshift of the excitonic resonance on a minute timescale. This leads to a decrease in the refractive index and tunes the BIC resonance by more than 40~nm in the visible range. Subsequent exposure to HBr vapor reverses the exchange process and restores the resonance to its initial spectral position. As a functional demonstration, we show continuous reversible spectral tuning of BIC-mediated lasing from the same metasurface. Our results establish gas-phase anion exchange as a non-destructive mechanism for continuous, reversible, and large-range post-fabrication control of perovskite metasurfaces, opening opportunities for chemically programmable lasers, adaptive meta-optics, and active nanophotonic devices.
