**Title:** Hybridization-Induced Enhancement of the Thermoelectric Response in the Kagome Material CsCr$_6$Sb$_6$

**Authors:** Hongye Zhang, Boqin Song, Aifeng Wang, Yisheng Chai, Xiaoyuan Zhou, Ying Zhu, Tianping Ying, Mingquan He

**Author_match:** True

**Abstract:**
> Flat bands in kagome lattices provide a prominent route to strongly correlated electronic states. Here we report charge and thermal transport measurements on single crystals of CsCr$_6$Sb$_6$, a kagome Kondo system arising from the hybridization between flat and dispersive bands. The Seebeck coefficient and Nernst signal increase rapidly below $T_\mathrm{F}\sim70$ K, concomitant with the emergence of short-range antiferromagnetic correlations and flat-band resonance. The Nernst coefficient in the intermediate-temperature range of $\sim30-80$ K further follows the universal scaling reported for prototypical heavy-fermion compounds, consistent with the development of strongly renormalized quasiparticles associated with hybridization. Both the Seebeck and Nernst responses reach extrema at a lower temperature $T_\mathrm{p}\sim20-25$ K, where phonon drag and correlated quasiparticles may both contribute. These results reveal a hybridization-induced enhancement of the thermoelectric response and establish CsCr$_6$Sb$_6$ as a kagome platform for probing flat-band-derived Kondo physics through thermoelectric transport.

**Title:** Robust Anomalous Hall Effect in Epitaxial Cubic D0$_3$-Fe$_3$Ge Nanoplates Grown by Chemical Vapor Deposition

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:berry curvature

**Authors:** Mingxuan Chen, Chao Chen, Siqi Jiang, Shuxiang Qiao, Hongyan Lu, Di Zhang, Jiabei Huang, Fuzhuo Lian, Zhaochen Qu, Siqin Wang, Ziyi Wang, Shuai Zhang, Yulu Liu, Ronghua Liu, Xiaoxiang Xi, Renjun Du, Lei Wang, Geliang Yu

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> The iron-based ferromagnetic materials have emerged as a promising system for spintronics due to their high Curie temperatures and significant anomalous Hall effect (AHE) driven by Berry curvature. While there have been investigations into the epitaxial growth of non-layered iron-based ferromagnetic materials, the realization of high-quality single crystal thin films continues to be a formidable obstacle. Meanwhile, the issues such as grain boundary scattering further hinder their characterization and application. Here, we report the first chemical vapor deposition (CVD) synthesis of single-crystalline cubic D0$_3$-Fe$_3$Ge nanoplates. This compound represents a substantial departure from the conventional hexagonal phases typically observed in the Fe-Ge system. Structural characterizations confirm the cubic lattice and stoichiometric purity of the crystals. Structural analysis identifies a strong epitaxial effect, resulting in a giant compressive strain of ~4.16% at the interface that relaxes gradually toward the surface. The second-harmonic generation (SHG) signals might further reveal that such interfacial strain disrupted the inherent centro-symmetry of the cubic phase. SQUID magnetometry shows robust room-temperature ferromagnetism and clear magnetic anisotropy. Magneto-transport measurements reveal a robust AHE that remains stable at temperatures far exceeding room temperature. The scaling observed between the anomalous Hall resistance and longitudinal resistance strongly suggests that this effect is dominated by the intrinsic Berry curvature mechanism. Furthermore, density functional theory (DFT) calculations reveal that the huge epitaxial strain induces band reconstructions and creates dense spin-orbit coupling gaps near the Fermi level, effectively doubling the anomalous Hall conductivity to match our experimental observations.

**Title:** Intertwined complex orbital order and ferrimagnetism in honeycomb molybdate Fe$_2$Mo$_3$O$_8$

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:berry curvature

**Authors:** Hong-Hao Song, Gang v. Chen

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Motivated by the recent experimental interest in honeycomb molybdates A$_2$Mo$_3$O$_8$, we return and develop the minimal models to capture the essential physics for Fe$_2$Mo$_3$O$_8$. It is pointed out that, the magnetism in these materials arises from the mutual induction between the spin-orbit coupling of the tetrahedral sublattice with active orbitals and the superexchange with the spins from the octahedral sublattice. Essentially, the superexchange between the tetrahedral and octahedral sublattices directly drives the ferrimagnetism, and the residual spin-orbit coupling on the tetrahedral sublattice induces the complex orbital order. The hybrid magnon-phonon excitations are further explored together with their resulting Berry curvatures and thermal Hall effect. We discuss the experimental relevance with Fe$_2$Mo$_3$O$_8$, and the framework can be extended to FeNiMo$_3$O$_8$, Ni$_2$Mo$_3$O$_8$ and other isostructural magnets.

**Title:** Synchronization-Induced Limit Cycles and Hysteresis in Spinor Exciton-Polaritons

**Keyword hits:** exciton:exciton, exciton:excitonic, exciton:polariton, exciton:exciton-polariton

**Authors:** Saeid Asgarnezhad-Zorgabad, Zahra Jalali-Mola, Vladimir V. Konotop, Daniel Leykam

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> We study the nonlinear dynamics of spinor exciton-polaritons in resonantly pumped microcavities. Using steady-state analysis and computing eigenfrequencies via Bogoliubov-like linearization, we show that multiple Hopf bifurcations beyond conventional bistability occur within specific power regimes and are not associated with stable solutions of the system. Owing to nonlinear excitonic interactions, these transitions occur at different pump powers during forward and backward pump sweeps, leading to pronounced hysteresis between fixed-point and limit-cycle states. In the spinor regime, multiple limit-cycle branches and associated dynamical hysteresis loops emerge due to the interplay of polarization coupling and spin-dependent nonlinearities. We show how the locations of the bifurcation points can be tuned by the pump frequency and other system parameters. Our results identify synchronization as the organizing principle underlying limit-cycle formation, dynamical hysteresis, and bifurcation cascades in driven spinor exciton-polariton microcavities.

**Title:** Interaction-Induced Bloch Geometry and Transverse Thermoelectric Transport

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:quantum metric, quantum_geometry:berry curvature, quantum_geometry:berry connection

**Authors:** Heng-Yu Di, YuanDong Wang, Zhen-Gang Zhu, Gang Su

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Electron--electron interactions can reconstruct Bloch wave functions, changing their geometry and the associated thermoelectric response. We derive the linear thermoelectric response within the Hartree--Fock (HF) approximation using the thermal length-gauge density-matrix approach, resolving Drude, symmetric quantum-metric, and antisymmetric Berry-curvature contributions. In a noninteracting Bloch eigenbasis, we separate the reconstructed Berry connection into the noninteracting connection and an interaction connection. This gives bare, coherence, and interaction-connection contributions to the anomalous thermoelectric response. We determine the constraints imposed by spatial inversion, rotational, time-reversal, and combined symmetries on the three transport channels. In the relaxation-time approximation, the leading weak-broadening behavior is $Γ^{-1}$ for the Drude contribution, $Γ$ for the quantum-metric contribution, and $Γ^0$ for the Berry-curvature contribution. The interband scalings apply away from band degeneracies. Self-consistent calculations for the triangular-lattice extended Hubbard model illustrate how interactions generate Berry curvature and a quantum metric, change the symmetry of the system, and thereby contribute to transverse thermoelectric transport.

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Optically addressable electron spin qubits that function at room temperature and under ambient conditions are essential for advancing quantum sensing. Solid state defect platforms offer robust spin-photon interfaces but lack precise defect positioning and electronic tunability. Here, we introduce para-halophenyl (-Cl, -Br and -I) quantum defects in (6,5) single wall carbon nanotubes that trap triplet excitons with a spin-photon interface that is operable under ambient, room temperature conditions. Here, we demonstrate optical initialization and readout of these triplet states at room temperature, provide proof of principle sensing of paramagnetic molecules, and highlight potential for on chip detection. Together, these results establish SWCNT defects as a promising new platform for future quantum sensing.

**Title:** Quantics tensor trains for multiloop functional renormalization group in multiorbital impurity models

**Keyword hits:** altermagnetism:altermagnetic

**Authors:** Hyun-Yong Lee, Naoki Kawashima

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> The full frequency dependence of electronic interactions is central to dynamical correlations, but its numerical cost restricts multiorbital calculations. We formulate the multiloop functional renormalization group directly in the quantics tensor train representation, retaining all three independent vertex frequencies over broad ranges without an explicit high-frequency parametrization. Analytical tensor operators perform frequency transformations and internal summations without dense vertex intermediates. Multiloop corrections improve agreement of vertices and responses with independent references and reproduce the low-energy numerical renormalization group self-energy. Lower-temperature calculations resolve a quadratic frequency contribution to the analytically continued scattering rate. A degenerate two-orbital impurity exhibits distinct spin, orbital, and charge responses at fixed Hund coupling. In a compensated altermagnetic environment, an orbital-level splitting induces a local spin response despite zero magnetization of the unperturbed impurity. Increasing Hund coupling suppresses the orbital response while increasing its spin polarization. Renormalized one-particle propagation alone does not reproduce this strong polarization dependence. These results extend compressed multiloop calculations to dynamical correlations in multiorbital environments.

**Title:** Symmetry-Guided Machine Learning of Spin-Dependent Nonlinear Hall Response

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:nonlinear Hall

**Authors:** Jyotirmoy Sau

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> A Berry-curvature dipole can produce a nonlinear Hall current even when the linear Hall response vanishes, but symmetry alone does not determine its magnitude or magnetic tunability. The interplay of structural symmetry, moment orientation, and carrier occupation is examined in a honeycomb model. Curvature maps and the band-summed dipole integrand reveal how velocity and the occupation window near the Fermi level select signed contributions. Removing threefold rotation permits a nonmagnetic dipole perpendicular to the surviving mirror. Generic magnetic reorientation activates the second component and tunes both components through positive and negative values. Global reversal preserves the dipole while reversing the intrinsic linear Hall conductivity, allowing a finite bulk dipole contribution from equally populated reversed domains under uniform-field averaging.A symmetry-constrained polynomial model predicts both dipole components for 256 held-out spin configurations, achieving coefficients of determination $R^2 > 0.9995$ at the reference chemical potential. At fixed carrier density, orientation-dependent shifts of the chemical potential modify the response and yield slightly larger prediction errors for the chosen model. Across the chemical-potential sweep,the prediction accuracy decreases more strongly for $D_y$,revealing an energy dependence of the surrogate's performance. Within semiclassical transport with a constant relaxation time, the calculated dipole determines the angular dependence of the quadratic current and its rectified and second-harmonic components. These results provide a quantitative representation of magnetic-orientation-dependent Berry-curvature dipoles and show how carrier occupation influences both the response and its predictability.

**Title:** Electrically Pumped Quadratic-Soliton Mode-Locked Laser in Lithium Niobate Nanophotonics

**Keyword hits:** soliton:soliton

**Authors:** Benjamin K. Gutierrez, Nicolas Englebert, Yingchu Xu, Selina Zhou, Maximilian Shen, Mahmood Bagheri, Alireza Marandi

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Femtosecond mode-locked lasers are the workhorse of modern photonics, enabling optical atomic clocks, frequency combs, precision spectroscopy, and ultrafast signal processing. Bringing them onto nanophotonic chips promises miniaturized ultrafast systems, yet combining femtosecond pulse generation with electrical pumping remains a major challenge, rooted in a trade-off between the speed and energy efficiency of mode-locking mechanisms. Kerr-based mechanisms offer nearly instantaneous response but typically require high intracavity peak powers that are difficult to achieve with nanophotonic-compatible semiconductor gain. Conversely, semiconductor saturable absorbers enable strong nonlinear responses at low energies but are limited by their slow carrier dynamics. Here we break this trade-off by introducing quadratic-soliton mode-locking based on a quadratic nonlinear interferometer (QNLI): an effective saturable absorber built from cascaded second-order nonlinearity in periodically poled lithium niobate nanophotonics, with a measured sub-20 fs response time. Through hybrid integration with an electrically pumped semiconductor gain element, we demonstrate a self-starting quadratic-soliton mode-locked laser with femtojoule pulse energies, operating in single- and multiple-soliton states. Quadratic solitons arise through the QNLI's dissipative and dispersive nonlinear response, which we confirm with a modified complex Ginzburg-Landau equation in agreement with measurements. By combining soliton formation with intracavity electro-optic modulation, we generate 750 fs pulses with flat-top spectra. Our engineerable, ultra-energy-efficient, electrically powered laser provides the critical source for fully integrated ultrafast photonic circuits in lithium niobate nanophotonics that natively offers electro-optic control, quadratic wavelength conversion, pulse compression, and spectral broadening.
