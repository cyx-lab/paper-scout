**Title:** Ferroelastic Reversal Magnon Thermal Hall Effect in a Fully Compensated Ferrimagnet

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:berry curvature, altermagnetism:altermagnetic

**Authors:** Yingxi Bai, Zihan Chen, Chengwang Niu, Ming Yang

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Magnons offer a charge-neutral channel for spin and heat transport, yet reversible and nonvolatile control of their thermal Hall response poses a fundamental challenge in systems with zero net magnetization. Here, we establish ferroelastic switching as an effective route to manipulate magnonic transverse responses in fully compensated ferrimagnets. A minimal Heisenberg-Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya model reveals that ferroelastic switching can re-orientate the lattice anisotropy, which reconstructs the chirality-resolved magnon bands and Berry curvature distribution. Consequently, the two orthogonal fully compensated ferrimagnetic variants related by [C2||C4z] exhibit magnon thermal Hall responses with opposite signs, whereas the response vanishes in the intermediate altermagnetic phase. First-principles calculations further identify monolayer V2SSe as a promising material candidate for realizing the proposed ferroelastic control of transverse magnon transport. Remarkably, ferroelastic switching drives a dual magnonic-electronic band reconstruction, enabling nonvolatile reversal of the magnon thermal Hall effect while producing anisotropic orbital Hall responses and distinct magneto-optical spectra as complementary transport and optical fingerprints. Our findings establish ferroelasticity as an effective route to nonvolatile magnon control and multichannel state identification in zero net magnetization systems.

## Rank #2

**Title:** Shaping Near Fields of Photonic Modes into Topological Textures

**Keyword hits:** exciton:polariton

**Authors:** Jonas Biechteler, Michael Hirler, Connor Heimig, Martin Barkey, Haoran Ren, Stefan A. Maier, Enrico Baù, Andreas Tittl

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Topological textures of light, such as optical vortices and skyrmions, are attractive for high-density information encoding and light-matter interactions. In the near field, topologies have primarily been realized using polaritonic platforms that inherently tie the platform to the material dispersion, limiting their footprint due to propagation losses and restricting their operating spectral region. Here, we introduce a route to shape near-field topologies through the geometric engineering of a dielectric metasurface supporting a quasi-bound state in the continuum resonance, without the need for polaritonic surface waves. In-plane deformation of our metasurface bars conserves their resonance-driven near-fields locally, so that the metasurface geometry and the polarization of the illuminating light dictate the generated topology. By shaping silicon unit cells into geometries with rotational symmetry, we create optical topologies with controllable topological charge. Using phase-resolved scattering-type near-field optical microscopy, we experimentally characterize high-purity resonance-locked topologies such as vortices in concentric rings and topological charge doublets in Archimedean spirals. In contrast to conventional topological textures, our purely photonic mode-based field textures are stationary standing waves in the radial direction, without relying on surface-wave propagation or polaritonic dispersion, establishing a spectrally and spatially scalable platform for near-field topological photonics.

## Rank #3

**Title:** The Mechanism of Chirality in Plasmonic Structures: Asymmetric Resonance Modes

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:circular dichroism

**Authors:** Abduweli Abdukerim

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> This study presents a theoretical framework for understanding the mechanism of chirality generation and circular dichroism (CD) in chiral nanostructures, with a particular focus on the role of asymmetric plasmon resonance mode distributions. Although previous studies have linked chiroptical properties to resonance differences, the specific nature and origin of these differences have not been fully elucidated. Here, we provide a more detailed explanation by quantitatively analyzing the asymmetric electric-field components arising from mode asymmetry. Specifically, we examine the near-field symmetry of individual nanorods and corner-stacked coupled structures (CSCS). The results demonstrate that chirality arises from asymmetric electric-field distributions, which give rise to distinct phase and amplitude responses of the electromagnetic fields in the composite structure under right-handed circularly polarized (RCP) and left-handed circularly polarized (LCP) illumination. The absorption and CD spectra, which exhibit distinct features around $910\,\mathrm{nm}$, support this mechanism and provide valuable insights into the design of chiroptical materials. The proposed mechanism is not restricted to a specific chiral structure or resonance and offers a broader explanation for the generation of circular dichroism.

**Keyword hits:** exciton:exciton, exciton:excitonic

**Authors:** Koushik Mondal, Amit Haldar, Indrajit Mondal, Arpita Dutta, Himanshu Bhatt, Biswajit Mahata, Soumili Banerjee, Hirendra Nath Ghosh, Satyaprasad P Senanayak, Shovon Pal

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Two-dimensional Ruddlesden-Popper-type halide perovskites have emerged as promising cutting-edge nonlinear optical materials due to their strong excitonic effects and carrier-density-dependent optical responses. In this work, we investigate the nonlinear optical properties and ultrafast carrier dynamics of (BA)$_2$PbI$_4$ under infrared excitation, resonant with the two-photon energy of its excitonic transition. Using a nonlinear femtosecond Z-scan measurement with a 1030\,nm pump pulse, we observe a pronounced intensity-dependent evolution of the nonlinear absorption coefficient and the refractive index. To elucidate the underlying carrier dynamics, intensity-dependent transient absorption spectroscopy was performed under the same excitation intensities. This reveals a long-lived charge carrier population above the estimated Mott density, reflecting a clear phase transition from an exciton-dominated regime to a dense electron-hole plasma regime. Moreover, such an intensity-driven, high, and switchable nonlinear optical response, along with a Mott-like transition under infrared pulse excitation, makes the 2D (BA)$_2$PbI$_4$ perovskites suitable for all-optical operation in the infrared regime.

**Title:** Chiroptical Valleytronic in SnSe

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:quantum geometry, quantum_geometry:quantum geometric tensor, quantum_geometry:circular dichroism

**Authors:** Yiming Pan, Sotirios Fragkos, Umberto Dellasette, Quentin Courtade, Dominique Descamps, Stéphane Petit, Fabio Caruso, Samuel Beaulieu

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Quantum geometric properties of Bloch electrons play a central role in governing light-matter interactions and transport phenomena in solids. Here, we report quantum-geometric chiroptical selection rules that generate a helicity-dependent momentum-space population imbalance in the group-IV monochalcogenide SnSe. By combining time- and angle-resolved extreme-ultraviolet photoemission spectroscopy with ab initio real-time simulations of photoexcited carrier dynamics, we identify valley-selective excitation at opposite crystal momenta arising from the breaking of time-reversal symmetry under circularly polarized illumination. We theoretically demonstrate that the magnitude of valley circular dichroism is strongly constrained by crystal symmetry through its influence on the quantum geometric tensor, the key quantity underlying valley optical selection rules. The resulting chiroptically induced valley polarization decays on sub-picosecond timescales through intervalley scattering mediated by electron-phonon coupling. Furthermore, we show theoretically that the population imbalance between valleys at opposite momenta gives rise to a helicity-reversible transient photocurrent. These findings provide microscopic insight into how quantum geometry governs valley-selective light-matter interactions, valley polarization, and photocurrent generation in van der Waals materials exhibiting hidden and transient symmetry breaking.

## Rank #7

**Title:** Anyon Chern bands of opposite sign and reversed drift

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:berry curvature

**Authors:** Zhenyu Xiao, Minxuan Wang, Shinsei Ryu

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> The elementary charged excitations of fractional quantum Hall states are anyons. Without continuous translation symmetry, they acquire a dispersion and richer dynamics. We study the Laughlin quasihole at filling $1/3$ in a periodically modulated magnetic field, a common model of fractional Chern insulators in two-dimensional materials. From the microscopic wave functions, we derive the commutator of its guiding-center coordinates, set by a many-body Berry curvature that governs its wave packets. A modulation with two flux quanta per unit cell splits the quasihole band into bands with Chern numbers $6$ and $-3$; such opposite signs are generic as long as a weak modulation opens a gap between the anyon bands. In the $C=-3$ band, the quasihole drifts against $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}$, although the magnetic field has the same sign everywhere. Perturbation theory in the modulation explains these results, and large-scale Monte Carlo calculations confirm them. Anyon bands thus acquire their own topology, which controls anyon motion and may shape the phases of itinerant anyons.

## Rank #8

**Title:** Impact of localized $4f$ magnetism on Berry curvature and the intrinsic anomalous Hall effect in CeRu$_2$Si$_2$

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:berry curvature

**Authors:** Takehiro Kobayashi, Shunsuke Hirai, Ibuki Terada, Michi-To Suzuki

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> The intrinsic anomalous Hall effect (AHE), originating from Berry curvature, has been extensively studied in $f$-electron systems, including Ce-based compounds. However, how the magnetism of localized $f$ electrons affect the intrinsic AHE remains unclear. Here, we investigate this issue in CeRu$_2$Si$_2$ by calculating the intrinsic anomalous Hall conductivity (AHC) for several occupied Ce-$4f$ states using first-principles LDA+$U$ calculations combined with Wannier interpolation method. We find that the intrinsic AHC remains finite even when the Ce-$4f$ states are localized and do not contribute directly to the Fermi surface, and that its magnitude depends on which Ce-$4f$ state is occupied. We show that localized $4f$-electron magnetism induces exchange splitting in the conduction bands, generating Berry curvature in specific regions near the Fermi surface. This mechanism enables conduction bands that are derived from nonmagnetic orbitals and form the Fermi surface to contribute to the intrinsic AHE, suggesting a route to realizing large intrinsic AHE in $f$-electron materials.

**Title:** Intertwined complex orbital order and ferrimagnetism in honeycomb molybdate Fe$_2$Mo$_3$O$_8$

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:berry curvature

**Authors:** Hong-Hao Song, Gang v. Chen

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Motivated by the recent experimental interest in honeycomb molybdates A$_2$Mo$_3$O$_8$, we return and develop the minimal models to capture the essential physics for Fe$_2$Mo$_3$O$_8$. It is pointed out that, the magnetism in these materials arises from the mutual induction between the spin-orbit coupling of the tetrahedral sublattice with active orbitals and the superexchange with the spins from the octahedral sublattice. Essentially, the superexchange between the tetrahedral and octahedral sublattices directly drives the ferrimagnetism, and the residual spin-orbit coupling on the tetrahedral sublattice induces the complex orbital order. The hybrid magnon-phonon excitations are further explored together with their resulting Berry curvatures and thermal Hall effect. We discuss the experimental relevance with Fe$_2$Mo$_3$O$_8$, and the framework can be extended to FeNiMo$_3$O$_8$, Ni$_2$Mo$_3$O$_8$ and other isostructural magnets.
