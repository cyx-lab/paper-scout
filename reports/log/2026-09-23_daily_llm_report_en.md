### Problem

Evaluating dense or fully connected long-range spin Hamiltonians on conventional hardware scales quadratically with system size and suffers from severe memory bandwidth bottlenecks.

### Approach

### One-sentence summary

The authors formulate a Berry-Landau Fermi-liquid theory that naturally integrates band quantum geometry into Landau theory, unifying interactions, quasiparticle occupations, and quantum-geometric transport.

### Problem

How to self-consistently incorporate band quantum geometry into Fermi-liquid theory and clarify how quantum geometry and Landau interactions modify longitudinal and transverse transport.

### Approach

Projected an interacting multi-orbital model onto an isolated band via Nozières-Luttinger construction to derive the energy functional with anomalous Berry-connection potential and geometric current, validated by finite-temperature Hartree-Fock calculations on a Wilson-Dirac model.

### Main takeaway

The intrinsic anomalous Hall conductivity is uniquely fixed by the Berry-curvature integral of the dressed quasiparticle band without backflow corrections, while the Drude weight contains an interaction-induced quantum-geometric current that dominates transport in flat/narrow bands and exhibits thermal enhancement.

### Why it matters

It bridges the foundational gap between Fermi-liquid theory and quantum geometry, offering a unified low-energy framework for transport in strongly correlated topological and moiré materials.
