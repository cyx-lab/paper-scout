### One-sentence summary

The study demonstrates that high-pseudospin fermions and enhanced quantum geometry induce a giant nonlinear Hall effect in strained, inversion-symmetry-broken twisted bilayer watermill lattices.

### Problem

Exploring topological phase transitions in high-pseudospin (spin-3/2) moiré superlattices and how to activate and enhance the nonlinear Hall response by breaking lattice symmetries.

### Approach

Using a continuum model to study twisted bilayer watermill lattices, introducing a mass term to break inversion symmetry and open a topological gap, and applying uniaxial strain to break threefold rotational symmetry to calculate the Berry curvature dipole and nonlinear Hall response.

### Main takeaway

The Berry curvature dipole in the strained twisted bilayer watermill lattice undergoes sharp sign reversals across topological phase boundaries and reaches magnitudes significantly larger than those in transition metal dichalcogenides, driven by its spin-3/2 character and enhanced quantum geometry.

### Why it matters

It extends moiré physics research from conventional spin-1/2 systems to high-pseudospin systems, proving that the nonlinear Hall effect is a highly sensitive low-temperature experimental tool for detecting topological phase transitions in time-reversal symmetric systems.

### Value assessment

### Problem

### One-sentence summary

This paper proposes two methods to directly and exactly extract Chern numbers from phase vorticities at the singularities of non-normalized Bloch eigenstates, demonstrated in a photonic lattice experiment.

### Problem

Traditional methods for measuring Chern numbers rely on approximate reconstruction and integration of the Berry curvature over the Brillouin zone, which is highly sensitive to experimental noise and only yields approximate integer values.

### Approach

Leveraging the non-normalization of wavefunctions in classical wave systems, the authors propose the '0-section method' and the 'tautological bundle method' to extract Chern numbers by identifying zeros of eigenstate components (or their ratios) and their phase circulations, experimentally demonstrated in a time-multiplexed photonic lattice.

### Main takeaway

Chern numbers can be exactly and robustly extracted by directly observing the zeros and associated phase vorticities of non-normalized Bloch eigenstate components (or their ratios) in the Brillouin zone, bypassing the need for complex Berry curvature integration.

### Why it matters

This approach simplifies the measurement of topological invariants from global Berry curvature integration to local observation of a few phase singularities, providing a highly precise and noise-robust topological characterization route for classical wave systems where eigenstate tomography is accessible.

### Value assessment

- **New model or method**
