### One-sentence summary

Quantum geometry converts exponentially small nonperturbative gaps in 2D quadratic band touching (QBT) systems into algebraically enhanced physical responses.

### Problem

Weak interactions at a 2D quadratic band touching (QBT) generate an exponentially small symmetry-breaking gap that is beyond all orders of perturbation theory and difficult to resolve.

### Approach

Utilizing the singular quantum metric in momentum space to convert the exponential dependence of the nonperturbative gap into an algebraic response via momentum integration, verified by optical sum rules.

### Main takeaway

Quantum geometry acts as an asymptotic amplifier of nonperturbative interaction scales, manifesting exponentially hidden gaps as algebraically enhanced signals in optical responses.

### Why it matters

This work reveals the universal amplification role of quantum geometry in correlated systems, providing a new optical pathway to probe elusive nonperturbative physical scales.
