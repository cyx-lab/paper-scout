- **summarized_at:** `2026-11-19T12:59:34`

### Related topics

### One-sentence summary

Demonstrated giant and fully reversible spectral tuning of bound states in the continuum (BIC) resonances and lasing in perovskite metasurfaces via gas-phase anion exchange.

### Problem

Traditional all-dielectric metasurfaces lack a method for dynamic, continuous, and large-range post-fabrication spectral control of their optical resonances like BICs.

### Approach

Fabricated lead halide perovskite metasurfaces using nanoimprint lithography and induced reversible halide ion exchange via alternating exposure to hydrogen chloride and hydrogen bromide vapors.

### Main takeaway

Gas-phase anion exchange induces a continuous blueshift exceeding 40 nm for BIC resonances in perovskite metasurfaces, which is fully reversible using HBr vapor and enables tunable polariton lasing.

### Why it matters

It provides a non-destructive, chemically programmable post-fabrication tuning mechanism that maintains optical responses without sustained external energy input, advancing active nanophotonics.

### Value assessment

- **Major advance**
- This work demonstrates for the first time large-range and reversible spectral and laser tuning in all-dielectric high-Q metasurfaces using gas-phase chemistry, overcoming traditional tuning limitations.

### One-sentence summary

This study proposes a microscopic quantum-mechanical model for a polarizability of laser-dressed quantum emitter ensembles incorporating pumping mechanisms, and solves Maxwell's equations via the Floquet formalism to reveal the rich band structure of dispersive time-varying media.

### Problem

Traditional time-varying media theories rely on classical electromagnetism and non-dispersive phenomenological time-dependent permittivities, largely ignoring the quantum-mechanical nature, dispersive response, and underlying pumping mechanism of the medium.

### Approach

By analytically modeling the polarizability of a homogeneous ensemble of quantum emitters driven by a strong pump laser and solving Maxwell's equations using the Floquet theorem to analyze the electromagnetic modes and band structures.

### Main takeaway

A dispersive time-varying medium formed by laser-dressed quantum emitters exhibits a rich band structure arising from the interplay between dispersive polaritonic and time-varying responses, featuring momentum gaps and exceptional points at band edges.

### Why it matters

This work bridges the gap between quantum microscopic systems and macroscopic time-varying optics, laying the theoretical foundation for realizing dispersive time-varying media and quantum light sources from driven quantum systems beyond classical treatments.

### Value assessment

## From geometric curvature to effective backgrounds: a dictionary for Dirac fermions in one, two and three spatial dimensions

- **summarized_at:** `2026-11-19T12:59:48`

### Related topics

### One-sentence summary

This paper derives a universal dictionary rewriting the Dirac equation on curved backgrounds in one, two, and three spatial dimensions as a flat-space equation with effective gauge fields.

### Problem

Dealing with the Dirac equation in curved spacetime for strained graphene and low-dimensional Dirac materials is cumbersome, lacking a unified and cross-dimensional flat-space translation framework.

### Approach

Based on a single Clifford-algebra identity, the spin connection is projected and expanded into trace and totally antisymmetric contractions, systematically tracking their manifestations across one, two, and three spatial dimensions.

### Main takeaway

The contractions of the spin connection correspond to distinct physical backgrounds depending on dimensionality: the trace term alone in 1D, a pseudoscalar mass term in 2D, and an axial vector potential in 3D.

### Why it matters

It provides a systematic flat-space calculation dictionary for Dirac fermions in curved geometry, facilitating theoretical analyses of graphene nanobubbles and 3D Dirac/Weyl semimetals.

### Value assessment

- **Mechanism clarification**
- This work clarifies the geometric effects of curvature and strain on Dirac fermions, systematically unifying the forms of effective background fields across dimensions.
