### One-sentence summary

Using a multiorbital Peierls-Hubbard model within the Hartree-Fock approximation, this study investigates the microscopic mechanism of coexisting structural and magnetic orders in solid oxygen, alongside their associated magnetoelectric, altermagnetic, and piezomagnetic responses.

### Problem

Solid oxygen exhibits a complex interplay between magnetism and structural distortions under pressure. The microscopic mechanism driving the magnetic-to-nonmagnetic transition, the competition among bond orders, and the cross-responses induced by symmetry breaking in coexistence phases remain poorly understood.

### Approach

A three-orbital Peierls-Hubbard model incorporating spin-orbit and electron-lattice couplings is constructed. The Hartree-Fock approximation is used to obtain the ground-state phase diagram, and Wilson-loop and linear-response methods are employed to compute electric polarization, band spin splitting, and piezomagnetic responses.

### Main takeaway

Antiferromagnetic order coexists with three distinct bond orders at intermediate correlations, where zigzag and meander coexistence phases exhibit linear magnetoelectric effects, while the tetramer phase exhibits altermagnetism with nonrelativistic spin splitting and piezomagnetism.

### Why it matters

This work provides microscopic theoretical support for complex magnetic and structural phase transitions observed in high-pressure solid oxygen, and uncovers novel multiferroic and altermagnetic signatures arising from magneto-structural coexistence in correlated molecular solids.
