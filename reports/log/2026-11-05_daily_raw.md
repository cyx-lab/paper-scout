**Title:** Geometric Pumping of Optical Work in Coherent Wave Scattering

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:berry curvature, thouless_pump:adiabatic pump

**Authors:** Zeyu Kuang, Konstantin Y. Bliokh, Stefan Rotter

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Optical work exchanged between light and time-varying matter underlies a wide range of emerging photonic technologies, yet a general geometric theory describing its generation and control has been lacking. Here we establish such a theory for coherent electromagnetic scattering. We show that the net optical work accumulated during an adiabatic modulation cycle is a geometric quantity determined by the Berry curvature of the outgoing field in parameter space. Nonzero optical work can thus be generated by cyclically modulating two or more independent system parameters. In contrast to conventional adiabatic pumping between incoherent reservoirs, optical work pumping vanishes under maximally mixed illumination. Greater illumination coherence increases the maximum work attainable through wavefront shaping. For fully coherent illumination, we derive the optimal incident wavefront and modulation trajectory that maximize the optical work per cycle. Our framework unifies time-varying photonics, optomechanics, and geometric transport within a common scattering formalism.

## Rank #2

**Title:** Longitudinal and Transverse Thermoelectric Properties in Topological Quantum Materials

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:berry curvature

**Authors:** Jyoti Duhan, Anil Kumar B. M., Satya N. Guin, Koushik Pal

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Thermoelectric materials can be topologically trivial or non-trivial, enabling reversible interconversion of heat and electricity using a solid-state technology. Incidentally, few of the first experimentally discovered 3D topological materials, namely, Bi, Sb, Sn and Pb-based chalcogenides, were long known as good thermoelectric materials. In these compounds, the heavy atoms that give rise to strong spin-orbit coupling often become essential for band inversion and non-trivial band topology. They lead to low lattice thermal conductivity, linearly dispersing Dirac-like bands induce high electrical mobility, and a band-inverted electronic structure with multiple band extrema gives rise to high Seebeck coefficients. In materials, where the applied temperature gradient induces electrical voltage (Seebeck effect) in the same direction, they are termed as longitudinal thermoelectrics. On the other hand, when the generated electrical voltage and applied thermal current remain orthogonal to each other, the materials are termed as transverse thermoelectrics. The transverse response can be driven by an additional external magnetic field (ordinary Nernst effect), intrinsic remanent magnetisation, or non-zero Berry curvature of a material, which arises from their non-trivial topology (anomalous Nernst effect). We discuss another class of quantum materials, known as goniopolar materials, that exhibit axis-dependent conduction polarity and hence transverse thermoelectric effects, which are based on mechanisms of longitudinal thermoelectric properties. In this review, we cover both types of thermoelectricity, focusing on materials that possess non-trivial topological order. We make connections between their structures, topology and transport properties and suggest tunability of thermoelectric properties going beyond conventional mechanisms such as electron-phonon interactions.

## Rank #3

**Title:** Evanescent-Depth-Engineered High-Q topological Cavities for Strong Photon-Exciton Coupling

**Keyword hits:** exciton:exciton

**Authors:** Zihan Mo, Renxuan Peng, Haoyang Zhang, Yu Tian, Ying Gu

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Due to the inherent scattering in topological edge states, the topological resonant cavity (TRC) formed by their interference exhibits a relatively low quality factor (Q). To address this issue, we propose the mechanism of engineering the transverse evanescent depth of edge states to achieve high-Q in irregular shaped TRCs, which further enables strong photon-exciton coupling at the single-quantum level. By adjusting the intra- and inter-cell coupling strengths in the pseudo-spin topological photonic crystal, the transverse evanescent depth of edge states is greatly reduced, thereby suppressing the scattering of TRCs. Consequently, the Q of various shaped TRCs with 2 ~ 3 um size is up to 10^5 ~ 10^6 in the visible, so that the strong photon-exciton coupling is achieved. The high-Q topological cavities hold promising application in integrated lasers, sensors, cavity quantum electrodynamics, and quantum photonics.

## Rank #4

**Title:** Hyperbolic Nanocavities for Single-Molecule Vibrational Strong Coupling

**Keyword hits:** exciton:polariton

**Authors:** Stavros Athanasiou, Olivier J. F. Martin, Francisco J. García-Vidal, Johannes Feist

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Hyperbolic phonon polaritons (HPhPs) in low-loss anisotropic materials such as hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) provide extreme sub-diffractional field confinement in the mid-infrared, making them attractive for enhancing the interaction of light with molecular vibrations. Collective vibrational strong coupling between molecular ensembles and HPhPs has already been demonstrated in extended slabs. Reaching this regime with a single molecule, however, remains a major challenge, and would provide a platform for investigating how vacuum electromagnetic fields influence molecular dynamics and chemical reactivity. Here, we show theoretically that single-molecule vibrational strong coupling can be reached in hBN nanocavities that laterally confine HPhPs within a finite volume. Combining full-wave electromagnetic simulations with the few-mode quantization approach, we find that these nanocavities support a rich set of asymmetric resonances that strongly enhance the local spectral density, with frequencies that follow from simple HPhP ray trajectories and can be tuned through the cavity aspect ratio. We identify resonances with quality factors of about $600$ and effective mode volumes of $|V_{\mathrm{eff}}| \sim 10^{-7}λ^{3}$, sufficient for single-molecule strong coupling with vibrational transitions with dipole moments on the order of $1\,\mathrm{D}$. These results establish hyperbolic nanocavities as a promising platform for single-molecule vibrational strong coupling and open new routes toward the local control of vibrational dynamics and molecular energy flow.

## Rank #5

**Title:** Colloidal 2D WS2 nanosheets exhibiting high-purity single photon emission

**Keyword hits:** exciton:exciton

**Authors:** Markus Xemaire, Jakob Keck, Marco Kögel, Marc Brecht, Jannik C. Meyer, Alfred J. Meixner, Jannika Lauth

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> The search for quantum emitters, delivering single photons "on demand" at cryogenic and elevated temperatures for photonic technologies is ever progressing. For example, micrometer-sized WS2 monolayers exhibit single photon emission by elaborated local defect or strain engineering. Here, we show that wet-chemically synthesised monolayered two-dimensional (2D) WS2 nano sheets (NSs) with lateral sizes of 14 (+/- 6 nm) readily emit single photons from exciton recombination without the need for substrate-induced patterning, strain/defect engineering or spectral filtering. Single particle spectroscopy of 70 individual NSs shows photoluminescence from excitons (2.02 eV), trios (1.94 eV) and defect-localized states (1.85 eV) with fast emission (t1<0.2 ns, t2=3.2 ns), allowing highly frequent excitation cycles. Pulsed at 80 MHz, colloidal WS2 NSs exhibit limited spectral diffusion, no blinking and distinct photon antibunching (g2(0)=0.07 at 5 K), thereby tackling challenges of established colloidal quantum dot systems (blinking, longer emission cycles), while retaining high single photon purity as key quality of deterministic quantum emitters.

**Authors:** Kevin Dini, Simone Zanotti, Timothy C. H. Liew

**Abstract:**
> Many NP-hard optimization problems can be mapped onto interacting spin systems, whose ground state encodes the optimal solution. Polaritonic implementations based on gain-dissipative dynamics have recently emerged as promising analog computing platforms owing to their intrinsic parallelism and ultrafast operation. However, existing approaches are hindered by two fundamental limitations: maintaining the homogeneous amplitudes required for faithful problem encoding remains challenging in realistic physical systems, and most architectures are restricted to nearest-neighbour interactions, severely limiting the variety of optimization problems that can be efficiently addressed. We introduce a feasible polaritonic XY simulator based on a reconfigurable waveguide platform that overcomes both limitations. Our approach guarantees amplitude homogeneity while enabling the implementa- tion of arbitrary symmetric coupling matrices, thereby allowing the direct encoding of a wide variety of XY Hamiltonians. These results establish a practical architecture for programmable polaritonic spin simulators and pave the way towards scalable optical hardware for analog optimisation.

**Title:** Phase matching and mode coupling in photonic-crystal ring resonators

**Keyword hits:** soliton:soliton

**Authors:** Alexander E. Ulanov, Lukas Bengel, Tobias Herr

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Photonic-crystal ring resonators, in which periodic refractive-index modulations couple counter-propagating modes, have become a widely used platform for nonlinear photonics, offering mode-by-mode dispersion engineering. When several modulation components are superimposed, their relative angular alignment (phase) is an additional degree of freedom whose impact on the nonlinear dynamics has remained unclear. Here we show that the relative phases between the modulation patterns have a decisive impact. Through the interference of the nonlinear processes in forward and backward direction, they add a phase and mode selection rule to the established phase-matching conditions, which can fully suppress nonlinear coupling. They also impose a relative spectral chirp between forward- and backward-propagating waveforms. We illustrate these effects for parametric oscillation and dissipative Kerr solitons. Our results extend the concept of phase matching to resonators with interfering nonlinear pathways and establish the alignment of index gratings as a design dimension for nonlinear microresonators.
