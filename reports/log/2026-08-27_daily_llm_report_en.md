### One-sentence summary

This work calibrates the phase of quantum oscillations in the ac magnetostrictive coefficient by directly comparing it with magnetization oscillations in the topological antiferromagnet YbMnBi2.

### Problem

The phase offset of quantum oscillations in the ac magnetostrictive coefficient has not been experimentally calibrated, limiting its use in extracting topological information like the Berry phase.

### Approach

Measure both magnetization and ac magnetostrictive coefficient quantum oscillations on the same YbMnBi2 single crystal, perform a direct phase comparison enabled by its single oscillation frequency, and analyze the results using Maxwell relations and LK theory.

### Main takeaway

The experiment confirms an approximate ninety-degree relative phase shift between the quantum oscillations of the ac magnetostrictive coefficient and magnetization, consistent with the thermodynamic Maxwell relation.

### Why it matters

This study establishes the experimental and theoretical calibration necessary for using ac magnetostriction to accurately extract electronic band topology information (such as the Berry phase) in topological materials.

### Problem

### Approach

Combining time-resolved photoluminescence measurements (using ring-shaped pulsed and Gaussian continuous-wave excitation configurations) with mean-field numerical simulations based on the truncated Wigner approximation.

### Related topics

exciton:exciton · exciton:excitonic

### One-sentence summary

Demonstrates that exciton-phonon coupling in polar semiconductors is governed by a parity-controlled interference selection rule, protecting same-parity ground-state excitons from long-wavelength polar phonons.

### Problem

Explaining why neutral excitons can be protected from the long-range infrared divergence of the Fröhlich interaction in polar semiconductors, and the variation in coupling strengths across materials.

### Approach

Performing an exact multipole expansion of the excitonic form factor combined with numerical integration of hydrogenic envelope functions across different dimensions and mass asymmetries.

### Main takeaway

Elastic scattering between same-parity states undergoes destructive interference that cancels the infrared divergence, while opposite-parity inelastic transitions exhibit constructive interference; mass asymmetry breaks the elastic channel protection.

### Why it matters

Provides a unified analytical framework explaining the weak phonon dressing in halide perovskites and strong sidebands in TMDs, offering design principles for engineering excitonic materials.

### Value assessment

- **Mechanism clarification**
- Clarifies the parity selection rule of electron-hole interference in exciton-phonon coupling, explaining the physical mechanism behind varying phonon dressing strengths in polar materials.

This study provides the first exact analytical expressions for the spectrum, eigenstates, and quantum geometric tensor of the quarter-flux Harper-Hofstadter model by exploiting sublattice symmetry.

### Problem

Deriving closed-form analytical expressions for the spectrum and quantum geometry in multiband models is challenging; previously, the quarter-flux Harper-Hofstadter model was only accessible numerically or perturbatively.

### Approach

By using a symmetric 2x2 magnetic unit cell to preserve sublattice symmetry in momentum space, the authors render the 4x4 Bloch Hamiltonian anti-block-diagonal, reducing it to a 2x2 singular value decomposition, and derive a general QGT decomposition for sublattice-symmetric systems.

### Main takeaway

The exact analytical eigenstates and quantum geometric tensors for all bands are obtained, analytically demonstrating that the lowest band is a nearly ideal Chern band favorable for hosting fractional Chern insulators.

### Why it matters

The Harper-Hofstadter model is a cornerstone for quantum simulation platforms like ultracold atoms and superconducting circuits; this analytical solution provides a precise theoretical benchmark for studying FCI stability and multiband quantum geometry.
