### One-sentence summary

This paper proposes a dynamical mechanism for transverse optical Hall rectification in asymmetric gold gratings based on nonlinear four-wave mixing and Kerr instability.

### Problem

Ideal one-dimensional asymmetric gratings are constrained by mirror symmetry, preventing the direct conversion of incident optical fields into transverse dc currents; the observed current requires an additional mirror-odd factor.

### Approach

A dynamical model is formulated involving a pump mode and a mirror-symmetric pair of surface-plasmon-polariton quasinormal modes, utilizing Kerr nonlinearity, four-wave mixing, and symmetry breaking induced by weak backscattering.

### Main takeaway

The transverse rectification current originates from a population imbalance in surface-plasmon-polariton modes driven by Kerr instability, where a small phase seed amplifies and selects the direction.

### Why it matters

This study provides a new symmetry-based framework for understanding nonlinear optical transport in nanostructures and reveals a mechanism for dynamical symmetry breaking in plasmonic systems.
