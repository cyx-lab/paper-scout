### Related topics

exciton:exciton · exciton:excitonic · moire:twisted angle · moire:twisted bilayer

### One-sentence summary

This study demonstrates that a perpendicular electric field controls exciton-dispersion anisotropy in valley-layer coupling TiSiCo to induce unconventional linear transverse exciton transport in monolayer and twisted bilayer structures.

### Problem

To explore how out-of-plane electric fields can control exciton transport and disentangle distinct transverse transport channels in valley-layer coupling 2D materials and their twisted bilayers.

### Approach

Constructed effective exciton Hamiltonians incorporating interlayer Förster coupling for monolayer and twisted bilayer TiSiCo, analyzed via semiclassical Boltzmann transport theory under the relaxation-time approximation.

### Main takeaway

A perpendicular electric field directly modulates exciton dispersion anisotropy, inducing anisotropic transverse conductivities in monolayers and tuning their interplay with T-even layer Hall/Nernst exciton counterflow in twisted bilayers.

### Why it matters

It provides a Berry-curvature-independent route for electric-field-controlled exciton transport, offering new opportunities for engineering excitonic devices using twist angle, temperature, and electric fields.

### Value assessment

- **Major advance**
- Extends the concept of valley-layer coupling to exciton dynamics, uncovering a novel mechanism of electric-field-tuned anisotropy-driven transverse transport and its interplay with layer counterflow in twisted bilayers.

### Paper type

### One-sentence summary

This paper reveals that the synchronization transition in coupled quantum self-sustained oscillators is controlled by cascades of Liouvillian exceptional points protected by generalized PT symmetry.

### Problem

To understand how classical phase-space topological changes manifest in the Liouvillian spectrum and slow modes of fully quantum coupled oscillators beyond semiclassical approximations.

### Approach

Formulated open quantum master equations for coupled lasers/polariton condensates, block-diagonalized the Liouvillian superoperator via symmetry sectors, and analyzed the spectral flow, Husimi Q functions, and PT-symmetry breaking under dissipative and reactive couplings.

### Main takeaway

Quantum synchronization is controlled by a cascade of Liouvillian exceptional points in slow modes, where different-order correlation functions synchronize at distinct coupling strengths, unlike semiclassical predictions of simultaneous coalescence.

### Why it matters

It establishes sharp, topologically protected experimental signatures for quantum synchronization in the few-particle regime, overcoming the smoothing effects of quantum noise on classical transitions.

### Value assessment
