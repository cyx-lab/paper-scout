### One-sentence summary

This paper presents a unified theory of magic angles in twisted bilayer graphene (TBG) by mapping the square of the chiral Hamiltonian to an electron moving in a non-Abelian SU(2) pseudo-magnetic field.

### Problem

Explaining why twisted bilayer graphene develops flat bands at specific magic angles and the physical origin of the magic-angle sequence, including the 3/2 rule.

### Approach

Using the chiral continuum model, the chiral Hamiltonian is squared to derive a Schrödinger-like operator for an electron in a non-Abelian pseudo-magnetic field, analyzed via energy sum rules, rescaling of the moiré potential, and Landau-level analogies.

### Main takeaway

The magic-angle sequence arises from the non-Abelian gauge structure under chiral symmetry; the 3/2 rule is determined by the rescaling of the moiré potential and its three-cell magnetic periodicity, with high-order zero modes behaving as coherent Landau states.

### Why it matters

This theory provides a unified mathematical framework for TBG flat-band physics, reveals a deep connection to the quantum Hall effect, and identifies topological phases with high Chern numbers and maximal quantum metric between magic angles.
