### One-sentence summary

This work reveals the competition and crossover between intrinsic (static chirality-driven) and extrinsic (fluctuating chirality-driven) mechanisms of anomalous Hall and Nernst effects in a 2D Kagome lattice.

### Problem

How to quantitatively disentangle and understand the distinct contributions of static spin configurations (intrinsic Berry curvature) and dynamic spin fluctuations (extrinsic scattering) to anomalous charge and thermoelectric transport in noncoplanar magnetic systems.

### Approach

Combining classical Monte Carlo simulations with a tight-binding model and semiclassical Boltzmann transport theory to separately calculate intrinsic transport from static spin averages and extrinsic transport from dynamical chiral fluctuations via skew scattering.

### Main takeaway

In magnetically ordered phases like skyrmion crystals, intrinsic Berry curvature dominates transport, whereas near the order-disorder transition, thermal fluctuations suppress intrinsic order and dynamical chiral fluctuations (extrinsic skew scattering) become dominant, with the two mechanisms contributing opposite signs.

### Why it matters

It provides a unified theoretical framework for understanding critical topological transport in frustrated magnets, explains experimentally observed sign reversals in topological Hall and Nernst effects, and guides the design of phase-transition-based spintronic devices.

### Value assessment

- **Mechanism clarification**
