### One-sentence summary

### Problem

Traditional quantum geometry (Kähler geometry) only describes isolated single bands with Abelian Berry curvature, failing to naturally capture time-reversal symmetric spinful crystals with Kramers degeneracy and non-Abelian SU(2) Berry curvature.

### Approach

By mapping Kramers pairs to quaternion projective space, the authors define a quaternionic quantum geometric tensor (QQGT) that unifies the quantum metric and three non-Abelian Berry curvature components, derive local metric-curvature inequalities from its positive semidefiniteness, and validate them using lattice models and DFT calculations of Na3Bi.

### Main takeaway

The ideal limit of time-reversal symmetric bands is governed by quaternion-Kähler geometry, where the saturation of local metric-curvature bounds defines non-Abelian ideal bands, offering a new geometric design principle for fractional topological insulators.

### Why it matters

It extends the paradigm of ideal quantum geometry from Abelian Chern bands to non-Abelian, time-reversal symmetric quantum matter, laying a theoretical foundation for designing and realizing fractional spin Hall effects and other strongly correlated topological phases in crystals.

### Value assessment

This paper introduces a beam propagation method based on discrete exterior calculus, achieving over 100x computational speedup for nonlinear optical waves while maintaining high accuracy.

### Problem

Traditional numerical methods face challenges of low computational efficiency, Gibbs oscillations, or difficult boundary conditions when simulating nonlinear optical wave propagation in structures with complex geometric boundaries and multiscale features.

### Approach

Utilizing discrete exterior calculus to directly discretize the nonlinear Schrödinger equation on unstructured meshes, constructing an explicit adaptive step-size propagation scheme by separating topological and metric operators.

### Main takeaway

The discrete exterior calculus beam propagation method eliminates Gibbs oscillations at step-index interfaces and runs over 100 times faster than conventional spectral methods.

### Why it matters

It provides an efficient, topology-preserving general computational framework for the rapid simulation and numerical optimization of multiscale nonlinear photonic devices.
