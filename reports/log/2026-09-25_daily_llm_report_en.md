### Related topics

### One-sentence summary

This paper demonstrates that electron-hole bilayers can exhibit an orbital inverse Faraday effect under circularly polarized light, driven by effective-mass asymmetry between carriers.

### Problem

In charge-compensated electron-hole bilayers, the orbital contributions from electrons and holes typically cancel out under circularly polarized light, preventing a net magnetization.

### Approach

The study employs a two-component semiclassical Drude model with interlayer Coulomb drag, complemented by a numerical and perturbative analysis of an excitonic model using the Rytova-Keldysh potential.

### Main takeaway

Effective-mass asymmetry between electrons and holes breaks the cancellation, allowing for a finite rectified orbital magnetization even in charge-balanced bilayers.

### Why it matters

This effect provides a non-contact, optical probe for investigating interlayer coupling, exciton dynamics, and carrier correlations in electron-hole bilayer systems.
