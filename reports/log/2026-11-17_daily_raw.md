## Rank #1

**Title:** High-quality intrinsic circular dichroism in silicon-based bound states in the continuum metasurfaces via strong coupling

**Keyword hits:** quantum_geometry:circular dichroism

**Authors:** Leyi Zhu, Tian Sang, Guanhua Yang, Chenyang Wang

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Bound states in the continuum (BICs) can significantly enhance spin-selective light-matter interactions. However, current BIC metasurfaces generally rely on single or multiple chiral BIC modes to enhance circular dichroism (CD), limiting their application in functional integrated devices. This paper proposes a method that utilizes the strong coupling effect in BIC metasurfaces to achieve intrinsic CD with both a high quality factor (Q-factor) and high efficiency. The metasurface is designed as a silicon-based cross-shaped nanohole array, which can simultaneously support a TM-like BIC mode and a TE-like guided-mode resonance (GMR) mode. By introducing under-etching to break the out-of-plane symmetry and tuning the in-plane asymmetry parameter, strong coupling between the quasi-BIC (QBIC) mode and the GMR mode can be induced, thereby achieving near-perfect intrinsic CD with a high Q-factor. In the strong-coupling region, the resonant wavelengths of the QBIC and GMR modes exhibit anti-crossing behavior, while the imaginary parts of the eigenfrequency cross; the corresponding Rabi splitting energy reaches a minimum of 3.16 meV, and the induced intrinsic CD achieves a maximum of 0.995 at a wavelength of 1653.9 nm, with a high Q-factor of 1083. The evolution of far-field polarization singularities shows that, under strong coupling, the Γ point corresponds to a left-handed circular polarization singularity (C-point). Furthermore, the strong-coupling-induced CD exhibits good robustness. Even under considerable variations in structural parameters, the high-Q and high-efficiency CD response is maintained. This method offers a novel design strategy for integrated chiral photonic devices and holds promise for applications in areas such as chiral sensing, chiral lasers, and nonlinear chiral optics.

## Rank #2

**Title:** Excitonic magnetism and competing valence bond solid instabilities in $d^4$ ruthenium compounds

**Keyword hits:** exciton:exciton, exciton:excitonic

**Authors:** Aleksandra Krajewska, Tomohiro Takayama, Hidenori Takagi

**Author_match:** False

**Abstract:**
> Spin-orbit entangled $d^4$ Mott insulators serve as a rich playground for unconventional magnetism and competing quantum electronic ground states. Intra-ionic spin-orbit coupling $λ_{\rm{SOC}}$ generates a non-magnetic $J_{\rm{eff}} = 0$ singlet ground state separated from an excited $J_{\rm{eff}} = 1$ triplet. The effective intra-atomic gap $Δ_{\rm{eff}}$ between the ground state and lowest excited state is determined by $λ_{\rm{SOC}}$ and non-cubic crystal field $Δ_{\rm{cf}}$. The intersite magnetic exchange $J_{\rm{ex}}$ induces dispersion of the excited $J_{\rm{eff}} = 1$ triplet. When the triplet bandwidth $W_{\rm{t}}$ overcomes $Δ_{\rm{eff}}$, the system undergoes long-range magnetic ordering which is viewed as a condensation of spin-orbit excitons and called excitonic magnetism. In this review, we systematically survey representative $d^4$ ruthenium compounds across four distinct structural connectivities of Ru-ligand octahedra, from K$_2$RuCl$_6$ with isolated octahedra, corner-sharing perovskite Ca$_2$RuO$_4$, edge-sharing honeycombs, to corner-sharing pyrochlores, and map them onto a conceptual parameter axis of $W_{\rm{t}} / Δ_{\rm{eff}}$. We demonstrate that $d^4$ ruthenium compounds not only represent evolution from a $J_{\rm{eff}} = 0$ Mott state to excitonic magnetism but encompass a broader competition of diverse electronic phases arising from spin-orbit coupling, exchange interactions, electron hopping, crystal field and bond covalency inhabiting similar energy scales. Near excitonic quantum critical point, where $W_{\rm{t}} \sim Δ_{\rm{eff}}$, lattice geometry and chemical bonds of constituent atoms play a decisive role in selecting the electronic ground state, frequently driving valence bond solid formation that pre-empts excitonic quantum criticality.
