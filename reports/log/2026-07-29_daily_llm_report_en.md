### One-sentence summary

This thesis investigates how superperiodicity, quasiperiodicity, and structural complexity shape electronic and spin transport in twisted multilayer graphene using large-scale quantum simulations.

### Problem

Exploring how geometry (moire superlattices and quasicrystals) and disorder jointly govern electronic localization, transport length scales, and spin lifetimes in graphene systems.

### Approach

Utilizing real-space linear-scaling Kernel Polynomial Method (KPM) based on tight-binding models to simulate moire twisted systems and transport evolution with millions of atoms.

### Main takeaway

In magic-angle graphene, moderate Anderson disorder counterintuitively increases the mean free path via disorder-induced delocalization; meanwhile, graphene quasicrystal resonant states are highly fragile against disorder and proximity effects.

### Why it matters

It reveals the non-intuitive role of disorder in flat-band systems and provides a microscopic mechanism for the discrepancy between charge transport and spin lifetime in suspended graphene.

### Value assessment

- **Major advance**
- Quantifies disorder effects on MATBLG flat-band transport and provides the first sub-ballistic exponent for graphene quasicrystals.

### Related topics

### One-sentence summary

Investigates the Aharonov-Casher phase in twisted bilayer graphene, revealing sharp peaks at magic angles due to Fermi velocity renormalization.

### Problem

Exploring the evolution of the Aharonov-Casher (AC) phase of electrons in twisted bilayer graphene (TBG) as a function of twist angle and electric field strength.

### Approach

Constructing effective Hamiltonians based on continuum models, incorporating Rashba spin-orbit interaction induced by a perpendicular electric field, and calculating the geometric phase generated by the SU(2) gauge potential.

### Main takeaway

The AC phase is linear with the electric field and exhibits sharp peaks at magic angles because the phase magnitude is inversely proportional to the renormalized Fermi velocity, which reaches a minimum at these angles.

### Why it matters

Provides a new quantitative probe for valley Berry curvature, quantum metric, and flat-band topology in TBG, proposing an experimental measurement scheme using double-tip scanning tunneling spectroscopy.

### Value assessment

- **Major advance**
- Connects a fundamental quantum phase phenomenon to magic-angle physics in TBG for the first time, revealing significant amplification of geometric phases by flat bands.

### One-sentence summary

### Problem

The influence of the quantum metric on nonlinear transport has been limited to non-centrosymmetric systems, leaving its role in the most common non-magnetic, centrosymmetric materials entirely elusive.

### Approach

Theoretically derived the third-order nonlinear conductivity driven by QMQs, and experimentally fabricated polycrystalline bismuth thin films to measure their third-harmonic response at room temperature using harmonic transport and terahertz spectroscopy.

### Main takeaway

Unlike second-order responses that cancel out due to domain averaging, third-order currents driven by quantum metric quadrupoles survive spatial averaging in polycrystalline films, serving as a robust probe of wavefunction geometry.

### Why it matters

This work extends quantum geometric transport to the most common centrosymmetric and polycrystalline materials, opening new avenues for highly efficient terahertz frequency upconversion devices under ambient conditions.
